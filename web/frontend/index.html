Results in context

These are retrospective, window-level results from four unseen individuals. High specificity coexists with a substantial false-positive burden and low precision. Window sensitivity is not event-level seizure sensitivity.

Examples illustrate both correct predictions and failures. Their frequency does not represent the test set. Neither model is calibrated, and these results do not establish broad clinical generalization.

Methods and limitations

One frozen, patient-independent study

CHB-MIT scalp EEG · 23 unique individuals · 15 training, 4 validation, and 4 held-out test individuals. chb01 and chb21 remain together as one person. This is one fixed split, not cross-validation.

683 eligible recordings, 18 canonical bipolar channels, 256 Hz, and nonoverlapping 8-second windows. Any positive overlap with an annotated seizure labels a window positive; incomplete trailing windows are excluded.

241,232 training windows · 51,969 validation windows · 147,727 test windows. Unsupported montages chb12_27, chb12_28, and chb12_29 are excluded: 10,824 seconds and 13 annotated seizures. Channels are never interpolated or zero-filled.

Validation-only selection

Both models use the same training-only normalization and frozen preprocessing. No additional filtering. Checkpoints maximize validation patient-macro AP; thresholds maximize validation patient-macro F1. The baseline remains the default based on validation AP.
